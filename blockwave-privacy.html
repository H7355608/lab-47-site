Data the game stores on your device

Blockwave stores your progress so that it is there when you return. It is held in the app’s private storage on your device and is never sent to us.

  • Your records — best score, best goal, longest chain, largest tile and total detonations.
  • Levels completed and stars earned.
  • Games played and a difficulty rating, so the game can match how you play.
  • A game in progress — the board, your tray and the score — so that an interrupted game can be resumed.
  • Sound and vibration settings, and whether you have seen the tutorial.
  • When an ad was last shown, so that ads do not appear too often.

None of this identifies you, and uninstalling the app removes all of it.

Advertising and the data Google collects

Blockwave is free and funded by advertising supplied by Google AdMob: a banner while you play, a full-screen ad between games, and an optional video you may watch in exchange for a second chance. You are never required to watch an ad to continue playing.

To select and measure those ads, Google’s software inside the app collects:

  • your advertising ID, a resettable ID Android gives apps for advertising;
  • your IP address, from which an approximate location such as country or city may be inferred;
  • device and app details — model, operating system, language and which app asked for the ad;
  • whether an ad was shown, seen or tapped, which is also how fraudulent activity is detected.

Consent in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland

If you are in the European Economic Area, the UK or Switzerland, the game asks for your consent before requesting personalised ads, using Google’s consent screen. You can accept or decline, and change your mind later. Declining does not limit the game; you will simply see ads that are not personalised.

Your choices

  • Reset or delete your advertising ID in Settings > Privacy > Ads on Android.
  • Turn off personalised ads in that same screen, or through the consent prompt where it is shown.
  • Delete everything the game has stored by uninstalling it.
  • Play offline. Blockwave is fully playable without a network connection; only ads require one.

Children

Blockwave is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them. The game has no accounts and no messaging, so there is no way to send us personal information through it. If you believe a child’s data has been collected, please contact us.

Legal bases in the EEA and UK

Where the EU or UK GDPR applies, we rely on your consent for personalised ads, collected as described in section 3 and withdrawable at any time, and on legitimate interests for non-personalised ads, fraud prevention and running the app.

You can ask to see, correct, delete or export personal data about you, object to how it is processed, or complain to your data protection authority. The ad data in section 2 is held by Google rather than us, so those requests are best sent to Google. We will assist where we can.

Your rights in the United States

Depending on your state, you may have the right to know what personal information is collected, ask for it to be deleted, and opt out of it being sold or shared for targeted advertising. We do not sell personal information. The advertising in section 2 may still count as sharing for targeted advertising under some state laws, and the Android settings in section 4 let you opt out of it.

Australia

Lab-47 is based in Melbourne, Australia. The law that applies here is the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and the Australian Privacy Principles made under it. Whether or not that Act applies to Lab-47 as a small business, we handle personal information in line with the Australian Privacy Principles, and this section sets out what that means for you.

What we hold. Nothing. As section 1 explains, your progress stays on your device, and the advertising data in section 2 is collected and held by Google, not by us. We do not sell personal information, and we do not disclose it to anyone in exchange for a benefit.

Overseas disclosure. Google processes advertising data on servers outside Australia, including in the United States. That is a disclosure by Google under its own privacy policy, not a transfer by us: we send nothing overseas because we hold nothing.

Access and correction. You can ask us what personal information we hold about you and ask for it to be corrected. Because we hold none, the answer will usually be that there is nothing to show. For the advertising data, the controls in section 4 are the direct route, and we will help you use them.

Complaints. If you think we have mishandled your personal information, email contact@lab-47.com with “Privacy” in the subject line. We aim to respond within 30 days. If you are not satisfied with our response, you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner.

Children. Section 5 applies in Australia as it does everywhere else. When the Children’s Online Privacy Code takes effect, we will meet it to the extent it applies to Blockwave.

Data breaches. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme requires organisations to tell affected people and the Commissioner about breaches likely to cause serious harm. We hold no personal information that could be breached. If that ever changes, this policy will say so, and we will notify you as the scheme requires.

Retention and security

We hold no personal data, so there is nothing for us to retain or delete. Google retains the advertising data under its own policies. Your progress stays on your device until you delete the app.

Ad requests use encrypted (HTTPS) connections. The game makes no other network connections.

Analytics and remote configuration Planned, not active

We intend to add two capabilities to Blockwave. Neither is active as of the effective date shown above.

Remote configuration would let us adjust settings such as difficulty or how often ads appear without releasing a new version of the game. The app would fetch a configuration file, which reveals your IP address to whoever hosts it, as any web request does.

Analytics would show whether a change improves the game. We expect to use Google Analytics for Firebase, which would collect an app instance ID identifying your installation rather than you, gameplay events such as a game starting or a level being completed, basic device and country information, and which variant of any test your installation was shown. Nothing further: no name, contacts, files, precise location or anything you type.

Before either is enabled we will update this page and its effective date, update the Data Safety section of the Google Play listing, and request consent where it is required. Until then, the device controls in section 4 apply to analytics as well as to advertising.

Contact and changes

For any question about this policy or about data in Blockwave, write to contact@lab-47.com.

If the game changes in a way that affects this policy, we will update this page and the effective date shown above, and note material changes in the app’s store listing. This policy applies to the Blockwave app only, and not to any site or service it links to.
