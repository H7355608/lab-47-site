The premise

One crate. One grenade.

Bangquet seats two to four survivors around a table in the wasteland and puts a crate between them. You draw, you look, and nobody else does.

What happens next is the whole game. Tell the truth and be disbelieved. Lie and get frisked. Level a revolver at the survivor opposite and demand they draw instead — the cylinder might be loaded, it might be empty, and only you know which.

The grenade goes to whoever runs out of nerve first.

The table

Read the room, not the cards

First person, across the table, in the dark, with Raider, Doc, Scav and Raven for company. Everything you know comes from three places: the parcel in your hands, the kit you were willing to spend, and the survivor who just told you the box is clean.

The kit

What you can bring to bear

Information is the only real currency at this table, and every piece of it costs something to buy.

The box

Sealed parcels, drawn blind. One holds the live grenade; the rest hold whatever the wasteland saw fit to pack. If you dislike the odds, you can put a round through the box instead of reaching into it.

The scope

Preview the next parcel before anyone else touches it. The knowledge is only worth what you can make the table believe.

The frisk

Search a survivor and expose an empty gun. A bluff survives exactly until someone is willing to pay to check it.

The sandbag

Absorbs a blast that was meant for you. One-time insurance against one very bad draw, and no help at all with the next.

The revolver

Level it and name a demand: draw from the crate, surrender the kit, or forfeit the turn. Loaded or empty, the demand looks identical.

House rules

The terms of the table shift between sittings. Learn the new rules fast, or lose to them before you have read them.

The standoff

Comply, jam, or call it

Every raised gun opens the same four-step exchange. Nobody at the table can see the cylinder, and everybody has to decide anyway.

Cost: your turn

Level the gun

Pick a survivor and name the demand — hand over, you draw, or sit out. Nothing about the cylinder is public, and nothing about your face has to be honest.

Cost: position

Comply

The target does as told. It is the cheap way out, and it walks the grenade one step closer to them.

Cost: kit, both sides

Jam

The target fouls the weapon and buys the turn back. It costs them something. It costs you more.

Cost: ammunition, or standing

Call it

They refuse and force the issue. If the gun is loaded, they wear it. If it is empty, you either spend ammunition or back down in front of the whole table.

Ways to sit down

Alone, or against people

Solo

2–4 players

Online PvP

Cross-platform tables against other players. Bluffs land differently when there is a person on the other end reading you back.
