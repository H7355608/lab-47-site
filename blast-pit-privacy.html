Blast PitPrivacy policy · v1.0

What Blast Pit knows about you.

Short answer: not who you are. There are no accounts and your progress never leaves your phone. What does leave it goes to three services: Google AdMob, for the ads that keep the game free; GameAnalytics, to see how runs play out and to fine-tune the game’s balance; and Google Analytics for Firebase, to learn which ads bring players in. One switch in the game turns both analytics services off. The long answer is below, in plain language.

Data the game stores on your device

Blast Pit stores your progress so that it is there when you return. It is held in the app’s private storage on your device and is never sent to us.

  • Your records and collection — best score, run statistics, a short list of recent runs, and the pieces you have made in each world.
  • What you have earned — coins, worlds owned and the one selected, permanent upgrades, rank and rank points, long-term goals.
  • Your daily streak — days played in a row, missed-day passes, today’s goals and the weekly challenge.
  • A run in progress — the pieces in the pit, the score and the upgrades taken — so that an interrupted run can be resumed.
  • Sound, music, vibration, motion, shot timer, play data and language settings, and whether you have seen the tutorial.
  • When ads were last shown and how many today, so that ads do not appear too often.
  • For the analytics in sections 3 and 5: the random player ID, a session count, any game settings received by remote tuning, and events waiting to be sent while you are offline.

None of this identifies you, and uninstalling the app removes all of it.

Advertising and the data Google collects

Blast Pit is free and funded by advertising supplied by Google AdMob: a full-screen ad between runs, at most every few minutes and never in a new player’s first runs, and optional videos you may watch for a reward, such as a second chance, double coins or an extra Blast shot. Rewarded videos are always your choice, and the full-screen ad never interrupts a run.

To select and measure those ads, Google’s software inside the app collects:

  • your advertising ID, a resettable ID Android gives apps for advertising;
  • your IP address, from which an approximate location such as country or city may be inferred;
  • device and app details — model, operating system, language and which app asked for the ad;
  • whether an ad was shown, seen or tapped, which is also how fraudulent activity is detected.

The game asks Google for ads rated no higher than Teen. This data is collected by Google and its advertising partners and handled under Google’s privacy policy; the partners are listed on Google’s ad technology providers page. We receive only aggregated reports: how many ads were shown and what they earned.

Play analytics: GameAnalytics

Blast Pit sends play data to GameAnalytics, an analytics service for games operated by GameAnalytics ApS. It shows us how runs play out — where new players stop, which upgrades are picked, how long a run lasts and what ends it — so we can make the game fairer and more fun. The game talks to GameAnalytics directly over HTTPS; there is no GameAnalytics software library inside the app.

What is sent:

  • a random player ID the game creates the first time it opens, a session ID and a session count. It is not your advertising ID and is not linked to it;
  • device and app details — platform, operating system version, device model and app version;
  • when a session starts and how long it lasts;
  • gameplay events: each run’s start and end with its score, how long it lasted, how many shots it took, the difficulty it reached and what ended it; the upgrade cards offered and picked; how often each tool was used; features unlocked as you progress; whether the tutorial was skipped; the shot timer, language and play data settings being changed; and which ads were shown or watched to the end, by placement;
  • if you are part of a test (section 5), which version of the game you were shown.

Your IP address is visible to GameAnalytics’ servers, as with any web request, and is used to work out your country. GameAnalytics handles this data for Lab-47 under its terms; its own policy is at gameanalytics.com/privacy. Events are sent in small batches about every 20 seconds; events from offline play wait on your device and are sent the next time you are online.

Analytics: Google Analytics for Firebase

Blast Pit also includes Google Analytics for Firebase, an analytics service operated by Google. It tells Google Ads and AdMob which installs and play came from our own ad campaigns, so we can spend on advertising sensibly, and gives us Google’s reports on how the game is used.

Through its software inside the app, Firebase collects:

  • an app instance ID, a random identifier created for this installation of the game, and your advertising ID;
  • events it records automatically: the first time the game is opened, each session and how long it lasts, and the app being updated or removed;
  • a short, fixed list of gameplay events: the tutorial begun, finished or skipped; a run starting and ending, with its score, length and cause; an upgrade card picked; a new feature unlocked; and a rewarded ad watched to the end. Two details are attached to the installation: how far along the learning path it is, and whether the shot timer is on;
  • the install referrer Google Play passes to the app, which names the ad campaign, if any, that led to the install;
  • device and app details — model, operating system version, language and app version — and your country or region, worked out from your IP address. Google Analytics does not store the IP address itself;
  • ad impressions and the estimated revenue they earned, as reported by AdMob.

Neither analytics service collects your name, email address, contacts, files, precise location or anything you type, and the game has no crash reporting.

Google processes this data for Lab-47, and we see it as reports in the Firebase console. Because the Firebase project is linked to Google Ads and AdMob, Google uses it to measure which ads led to installs and play. Google’s description of how Firebase handles data is at firebase.google.com/support/privacy.

Remote tuning and tests

When a session starts, the game asks GameAnalytics for any balance settings we have published — for example how fast the back wall moves or how often events happen — so the game can be fine-tuned without a new release. New settings take effect from your next run, never in the middle of one, and values outside the game’s safe limits are ignored.

Occasionally we may run a test: installations are split at random between two versions of a setting, and we compare how they play. The only data this adds is which version your installation was shown, attached to the events in section 3. No test changes what you pay, what ads you see or how your data is handled.

Remote tuning is part of play analytics: when you turn off Share play data, the game stops asking for new settings and plays with the ones it already has.

Consent

If you are in the European Economic Area, the UK or Switzerland, or in a place where the law asks for one, the game shows Google’s consent screen before it requests personalised ads. You can accept or decline. Declining does not limit the game; you will simply see ads that are not personalised.

Analytics wait for that screen. Nothing is sent to GameAnalytics or Firebase until the consent screen has been answered, where it is shown. Google’s consent screen records your answer in the industry-standard IAB TCF format, which Google’s software in the app, Firebase included, reads and respects for the advertising uses of its data.

Where the law gives you that right, the game’s Settings show a Privacy choices row that reopens the consent screen, so you can change your answer at any time.

Your choices

  • Turn analytics off in the game. In Settings, switch off Share play data. From then on nothing is sent to GameAnalytics or Firebase, events waiting to be sent are deleted from your device, and remote tuning stops. Switch it back on at any time.
  • Reset or delete your advertising ID in Settings > Privacy > Ads on Android. Deleting it stops AdMob and Firebase from using it.
  • Turn off personalised ads in that same screen, or through the consent screen where it is shown (section 6).
  • Delete everything the game has stored by uninstalling it. A new install starts with new identifiers, unlinked to the old ones.
  • Ask for analytics data to be deleted by writing to contact@lab-47.com. Lab-47 will ask GameAnalytics and Google to delete data about your installation, as far as it can be found.
  • Play offline. Blast Pit is fully playable without a network connection. Only ads, analytics and remote tuning need one.

Children

Blast Pit is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them. The game has no accounts and no messaging, so there is no way to send us personal information through it. If you believe a child’s data has been collected, please contact us.

Legal bases in the EEA and UK

Where the EU or UK GDPR applies, we rely on:

  • your consent for personalised ads and for the advertising uses of Firebase data, collected as described in section 6 and withdrawable at any time;
  • legitimate interests for non-personalised ads, fraud prevention and running the app, and for understanding how the game is played and tuning its balance (sections 3 to 5), with the Share play data switch letting you object at any time.

You can ask to see, correct, delete or export personal data about you, object to how it is processed, or complain to your data protection authority. Write to us for anything in sections 3 to 5, and we will take it up with GameAnalytics and Google. The ad data in section 2 is held by Google itself, so requests about it are best sent to Google. We will assist where we can.

Your rights in the United States

Depending on your state, you may have the right to know what personal information is collected, ask for it to be deleted, and opt out of it being sold or shared for targeted advertising. We do not sell personal information. The advertising in section 2, and the ad measurement in section 4, may still count as sharing for targeted advertising under some state laws. The consent screen, where it is shown, and the Android settings in section 7 let you opt out of both.

Australia

Lab-47 is based in Melbourne, Australia. The law that applies here is the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and the Australian Privacy Principles made under it. Whether or not that Act applies to Lab-47 as a small business, we handle personal information in line with the Australian Privacy Principles, and this section sets out what that means for you.

What we hold. Nothing on our own systems. Your progress stays on your device (section 1), and the advertising data in section 2 is collected and held by Google. The analytics data in sections 3 to 5 is held by GameAnalytics and Google for Lab-47, and we see it as reports in their dashboards, keyed to random install IDs and, for Firebase, advertising IDs rather than to names. We do not sell personal information, and we do not disclose it to anyone in exchange for a benefit.

Overseas disclosure. GameAnalytics ApS and Google process data on servers outside Australia, including in the European Union and the United States. Advertising data is handled under Google’s own privacy policy; analytics data is handled for Lab-47 under GameAnalytics’ and Google’s data protection terms.

Access and correction. You can ask us what personal information we hold about you and ask for it to be corrected. Because analytics data is keyed to random install IDs rather than names, we usually cannot connect it to you without more detail from you. For the advertising data, the controls in section 7 are the direct route, and we will help you use them.

Complaints. If you think we have mishandled your personal information, email contact@lab-47.com with “Privacy” in the subject line. We aim to respond within 30 days. If you are not satisfied with our response, you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner.

Children. Section 8 applies in Australia as it does everywhere else. When the Children’s Online Privacy Code takes effect, we will meet it to the extent it applies to Blast Pit.

Data breaches. The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme requires organisations to tell affected people and the Commissioner about breaches likely to cause serious harm. We hold no personal information on our own systems. If GameAnalytics or Google reports a breach affecting Blast Pit players, we will notify you as the scheme requires.

Retention and security

We keep no personal data on our own systems. Google keeps Firebase event data for the retention period set on Blast Pit’s Analytics property, at most 14 months, after which only aggregated reports remain. GameAnalytics keeps play data, and Google keeps advertising data, under their own policies. Your progress stays on your device until you delete the app.

Ads, analytics and remote tuning all use encrypted (HTTPS) connections, and every batch of play data is signed so it cannot be altered on the way. The game makes no other network connections.

Contact and changes

For any question about this policy or about data in Blast Pit, write to contact@lab-47.com.

If the game changes in a way that affects this policy, we will update this page and the effective date shown above, and note material changes in the app’s store listing. This policy applies to the Blast Pit app only, and not to any site or service it links to.
